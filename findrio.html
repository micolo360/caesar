A new kind of search, explained in one scroll

Findrio tracks how AI assistants describe a brand: whether it gets mentioned, how it ranks against competitors, and whether the facts are right. It is a new category. Most visitors arrive knowing SEO and not AEO, so the site had to teach the idea and earn a waitlist sign-up in the same visit.

Mantas, Findrio’s CEO, brought me in to update the landing page. It had started life on Lovable, and the team wanted something they could keep editing as the product took shape.

Moving to Framer so the team owns it

I rebuilt the site in Framer. The product changes week to week, and Framer lets the team change copy, swap a feature or add an update without waiting on a developer. It also gave us smooth motion and fast hosting out of the box.

I designed it twice. The first Framer version, shown above, led with a purple ribbon and market stats. The second, which is live today, kept what worked, like the market-by-market idea and the FAQ, and replaced the parts that felt generic with a stronger story.

A hero you walk into

The new hero is a figure walking down a swirling tunnel of light, under the headline “Improve your AI visibility.” It says “a new channel you can step into” faster than any diagram, and the single Join Waitlist button sits right in the middle of it.

The navigation is cut to three links and one pill button, so there is only ever one obvious next step.

Six features, one card at a time

Findrio does a lot: Monitor, Analyse, Optimize, Agentic Commerce, Agent and Content Studio. Listing all six at once would have been a wall of text, so each one gets its own numbered cream card that slides in over the black page as you scroll.

Every card follows the same pattern: a number, a one-line promise, three short proof points and an abstract artwork. People can skim the headlines or stop and read, and the page never feels heavier than one card.

Proof for enterprise buyers

Larger brands care about one thing marketers forget: AI gives different answers in different countries. The Enterprise Capability section uses a real-world example, a brand that operates in 83 countries, to show why tracking every market matters.

After that, a four-step “AI engine” diagram takes the mystery out of setup: connect your data, see your visibility, get recommendations, grow your presence.

Answering the objections before sign-up

The FAQ handles what stops people joining a waitlist: what Findrio is, how tracking works, which platforms it covers, how AEO differs from SEO, how fast results come and whether you need technical skills.

The page then closes on a dusk-lit “AI visibility improving hub” panel and one last Join Waitlist button, so a visitor who scrolled to the end never has to scroll back up.
