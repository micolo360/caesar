Trust is the whole brief

People look for a lawyer when something important is at risk: a dispute, a contract, a property. They are anxious, and they decide fast. The site had to make Sandra feel established, calm and personal within a few seconds.

It also needed to launch properly, with content Sandra could post to announce it, not just a URL.

Black, white and a lot of air

I kept the palette to black and white, with an elegant serif for headlines and generous spacing. It reads like a well-made legal document: serious, but never cold.

The hero opens with a portrait of Sandra in her wig and gown under the words “I am Sandra ESQ.”, followed by the line that sets the tone for the whole site: “When everything is on the line, you need more than a lawyer.”

Showing experience without bragging

Before visitors read her story, a short row of figures shows years of practice, cases resolved and business clients served. The About section follows with her journey and a quiet black-and-white portrait.

Classical imagery, like Lady Justice and courthouse columns, appears in small doses so the site feels rooted in the profession without turning into a cliché.

Making the practice easy to scan

The four practice areas, Commercial Disputes, Corporate Advisory, Property Matters and Dispute Resolution, sit side by side as photo cards, so a visitor can find their problem in one glance.

Below them, “Why clients choose me” and three guiding principles answer the real question every client has: will this person listen to me and actually get it done?

Proof, insight, then one clear ask

Client testimonials sit on light cards that stand out against the black. An Insights section with short articles shows how Sandra thinks, and an FAQ answers the questions people are too nervous to ask on a first call.

Everything leads to one action: book a consultation. It appears in the navigation, in the hero and again in a full-width illustrated panel near the end.

A launch people actually see

A new website only works if people visit it. I turned the site into an animated showreel that walks through every section, then cut a 15-second vertical version with music for Instagram Reels, so Sandra could announce the launch where her audience already spends time.

What went live
