Starting from one blurry file

The Cadillacs came to me with a single low-resolution white logo. It looked fine as a profile picture and fell apart anywhere bigger. Before any branding could happen, the mark itself had to be rebuilt so it stayed sharp at any size.

The goal after that was consistency: whatever the team posted, it had to look like it came from the same company.

A mark with character

The wordmark has a heavy, cinematic feel with tall, condensed letters, a little old-Hollywood and a little rebellious. I kept that personality and gave it two simple colourways, black on white and white on black, so it works on almost any surface without extra rules.

Proving it in the real world

A logo is only as good as the places it lives. I built a brand reel that puts the mark on a billboard, a shop sign, a metal card, an app icon, caps, ID badges, street posters and a full stationery set, cut fast to music for social.

It doubles as a brand guideline the team can actually feel: this is what we look like everywhere.

A LinkedIn series with one rule

For social, I made a series of ten flat illustrated animations about ideas the brand stands for: leadership, consistency, personal growth, self-reflection and preparing for a new week. Each one tells a tiny story and ends the same way, with The Cadillacs logo revealed inside the scene.

Some examples of how the logo arrives:

  • Far away on the horizon of an explorer’s binoculars, growing closer as the camera flies past
  • Revealed on the orange triangle a team is stacking together
  • At the end of the tunnel a walking figure is heading towards
  • Rolling out of a funnel machine as the final ball, instead of just popping up
  • White on red at the end of a leader guiding a group of followers

Because every piece ends on the logo in the same way, people start recognising the brand before they read the name.

What went live

  • Rebuilt, high-resolution logo in black and white versions
